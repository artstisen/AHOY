
*** Password Strength Indicator ***

N/A: No password defined.
Invalid: Inherently insecure/Contains whitespace/Illegal characters.
Weak: Very easy to crack (no security).
Moderate: Easy or difficult to crack (low security).
Strong: Difficult or impossible to crack (high security).
Extreme: Theoretically impossible to crack (highest security).
Indicators: "<" = Weaker variation.
            ">" = Stronger variation.

TIP: Use the password "test" for testing purposes. This particular string is not blacklisted and won't be deprecated by the Indicator.

ATTENTION: The indicator is a guideline following a set of rules that can't take every situation into account. If for example your name is John and you were born in 1965, then you can easily create what seems to be a moderate password by typing "John1965!". This is not a secure password although it matches the basic principles for a strong password. The indicator knows that a birth year should be avoided, but it doesn't know your name and its connection with your birth year, and will only give you penalty points for typing a year between 1900 and the current year. In reality this would be considered a weak password. Use the examples below to aid you in creating a strong password:

A strong password should consist of at least 8 characters, containing both upper- and lowercase alphabetic characters plus one or more numbers and symbols. Dictionary words, family names and birth year/dates etc. should always be avoided. Whitespace characters are not allowed.
Strong password example: "qZ*J5y+D".

An extreme password should consist of at least 12 different characters (Two or more of the same character is not permitted except for characters with different casing ("S" and "s")), containing both upper- and lowercase alphabetic characters plus one or more non repeating numbers and symbols. Whitespace characters are not allowed.
Extreme password example: "cSy1}~4;NV0q$lPm".

TIP: The Ahoy Password Generator is able to provide a range of randomly generated password types which complies to the above rules for strong and extreme passwords.

INFO: Attempting to crack passwords by trying as many possibilities as time can permit is a brute force attack. Another method, often more efficient, is a dictionary attack. In a dictionary attack, all words in one or more dictionaries are tested. Lists of common passwords are also typically involved.
Password strength is the crucial factor when a password cannot be guessed or discovered, and varies with the attack algorithm used. Cryptologists and computer scientists often refer to the strength or "hardness" in terms of entropy.
